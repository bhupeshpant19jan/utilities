First run: guided setup

Before searching, the skill shows suggested defaults and asks the user to accept or change them. DFW settings are examples, not universal recommendations.

Minimum inputs
  1. Market: metro/region and target cities or ZIP codes.
  2. Budget: maximum purchase price and maximum total cash per property, including down payment, closing costs, repairs and reserves.
  3. Strategy: investment horizon and objective — cash flow, appreciation, or balanced.
  4. Financing: down payment, interest rate and loan term, or permission to use current market-based estimates clearly labeled as assumptions.
  5. Must-haves: property types and any school-quality, rental-use or other non-negotiable gate.

The user can say “Accept the suggested defaults” and provide only missing financing details. The wizard asks only for essential gaps, reads back the confirmed profile, and saves it as investor_profile.json where persistent storage is supported. Later runs reuse the profile.

Example DFW defaults: $350,000 purchase-price ceiling; $150,000 initial-cash ceiling; 10-year horizon; balanced strategy; 7.0/10 minimum score; assigned elementary and middle schools each 8/10+; DSCR 1.00+ baseline; cap rate 6.0%+ preferred; and maximum $300/month owner contribution. DSCR 0.95–0.99 and cap rate 5.5–5.99% are conditional ranges, not automatic passes.

How to interpret the output

Label / metricMeaning
PASSMeets configured hard gates and baseline financial rules. Normal due diligence is still required.
CONDITIONALCould qualify only after a stated change, such as additional down payment. It is not a pass yet.
WATCHLIST / NEAR-MISSResearch lead that failed a gate or needs verification; not a recommendation.
Score (0–10)Weighted comparison of rental economics, cash return, risk, demand, appreciation and other factors. A high score cannot override a failed hard gate.
NOIRental income after operating expenses, before mortgage payments.
Cap rateNOI ÷ purchase price. Property-level operating yield; independent of financing. Higher is generally better, all else equal.
DSCRNOI ÷ annual mortgage principal-and-interest payments. 1.00 means NOI exactly covers that debt service; above 1.00 gives more coverage.
Monthly cash flowRemaining cash after operating costs, mortgage payments and stated reserves. Positive is preferable; negative means owner contribution.
Cash-on-cash returnAnnual reserve-adjusted cash flow ÷ total cash invested. Compare only with consistent assumptions.
Total initial cashEstimated cash needed to acquire and prepare the property, including down payment, closing costs, repairs and reserves.
Keep the metrics distinct. Cap rate, DSCR and cash flow answer different questions. A property may have a reasonable cap rate but weak DSCR because of its loan terms. Any conditional case should show the extra down payment and its effect on cash invested and return. School ratings must be for the exact assigned schools, not nearby schools or district averages.

Daily report or detailed analysis?

The daily report stays brief: executive summary, top 3–5 candidates, key metrics and risks, plus a small watchlist. Ask for a detailed property analysis for comparable rents and sales, expense assumptions, financing alternatives, stress tests, long-term projections, payoff scenarios, due-diligence questions and offer-price analysis.

Using it in another market

Install the complete folder and run the setup wizard. Replace DFW with the investor's actual market and limits. Verify local school assignment sources, taxes, insurance, zoning, rental rules, HOA restrictions and data availability. Do not assume DFW thresholds are suitable elsewhere.

For research support only. Not a buy/sell decision or a substitute for professional legal, tax, lending, inspection or insurance advice.
